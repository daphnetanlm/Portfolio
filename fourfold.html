CASE STUDY · FOURFOLD

From a rejected "brain dump" app to an approved journal and planner.

How I scoped, built and repositioned FourFold on my own, and got it through App Store review on the second attempt.

View on the App Store ↗

ROLE

Solo: product, design, build, listing and launch

TIMELINE

Sept 2026 build → live 16 Nov 2026

STACK

Swift, SwiftUI, SwiftData, CloudKit, EventKit, StoreKit 2

HOW

Built with AI-assisted coding in Xcode; Notion as the single source of truth

01

The problem

Tasks, half-made decisions, ideas and worries all end up in the same notes app, or nowhere. I wanted one daily view that sorts what's on your mind by what it actually is, so each thing gets the right next step.

The answer was four fixed sections: To-Dos to get done, Decisions to think through, Ideas to give a first step, and Let Go for things to stop carrying.

02

Reading the market

Before building, I mapped the category. At least seven App Store apps used "brain dump" in their name. Most were single-stream capture with AI sorting or voice notes on top, and most charged a subscription.

So I positioned FourFold on what they didn't do:

  • A fixed structure by type of thought, not free-form notes with tags
  • Fully manual and private: no AI, no account, data only on the phone and the user's own iCloud
  • Calendar on the same page, with entries that can be scheduled and moved
  • A five-day free trial, then a one-time purchase in a subscription-heavy category

03

Building in phases

I ran the build like a small Scrum project: one phase at a time, each tested before the next.

PHASE 1

Data model, on-device storage and iCloud sync first, so nothing built later could lose entries

PHASE 2

The daily view, mood check-in, diary, reminders, Face ID lock and dark mode

PHASE 3

Free trial and one-time purchase with StoreKit 2, synced across the user's devices

PHASE 4

"First step" scheduling for Ideas, two-way calendar, subtasks, moving entries between days and sections

I used the app daily while building it. When I lost diary entries by forgetting to tap Save, autosave jumped to the top of the backlog.

04

The setback: rejected as "spam"

In September, Apple rejected the first submission under Guideline 4.3(a): too similar to apps already on the Store. In a crowded category, a "brain dump" name made it look like one more of the same.

I found the closest competitor and compared the two honestly. Its "Let go" bucket was the nearest overlap, so I didn't claim that as a difference. I built the case on what really was different: manual entry instead of AI sorting, sections by type instead of urgency, a diary with photos and mood, and native calendar sync.

Then I changed the product, not just the wording:

  • Dropped "Brain Dump" and renamed the listing FourFold: Journal & Planner
  • Widened the scope into a full journal and planner: two-way calendar, subtasks, a richer diary
  • Withdrew the old build and resubmitted on 29 October with new screenshots, listing copy and review notes

Approved and live on 16 November 2026.

05

What it taught me

Positioning is part of the product. The rejection wasn't about code. It was about how the app read next to everything else on the shelf.

Be honest about overlap. Admitting where we were similar made the real differences more credible.

Keep one source of truth. Moving every decision, checklist and review note into one Notion workspace kept a solo project on track through two submissions.

06

What's next

Version 1.1 will add a Home Screen widget and Shortcuts, so entries can be added without opening the app, while still respecting the app lock and keeping private entries off the Lock Screen. iPad and Mac versions come after that.
