CASE STUDY · COFFEE BEANS JOURNAL

Snap the bag, remember the brew.

How a personal coffee log became my first App Store app, and the cost and privacy decision that shaped it.

View on the App Store ↗ Instagram ↗

ROLE

Solo: product, design, build, listing and marketing

TIMELINE

Live 15 Oct 2026 · v1.3 on 18 Nov 2026

STACK

React Native, Expo, Apple Vision (on-device), iCloud, StoreKit

MODEL

Free for five beans and two brews per bean; one-time unlock for unlimited use

01

The problem

As a home espresso brewer, I kept forgetting which beans I'd bought, what I paid and, most of all, how I brewed the good ones. The details were printed on the bag, and the bag went in the bin.

The goal: keep every bag in one place, and make logging it take seconds, not a form.

02

Iterating to v1

It started as a small web app (React, Tailwind and a browser database). To put it in people's pockets, I rebuilt it in React Native with Expo for the App Store.

The name went through four rounds: Roast Log, then Cupboard (dropped because the name was taken), then Roast Diary, and finally Coffee Beans Journal, which says exactly what it is.

03

The key decision: read the label on the phone

The headline feature is "snap the bag": photograph the packaging and the app fills in the name, origin, roast, process, weight and flavour notes. My first version sent the photo to a cloud AI model. Before launch, I costed it out and reviewed the risks.

BEFORE · CLOUD AI

  • About US$0.009 per scan (my estimate), growing with every user, against a one-time purchase
  • An API key inside the app that anyone could extract
  • Users' photos sent to a third party

AFTER · ON-DEVICE

  • Apple's on-device text recognition: no cost per scan
  • No key to leak, no server to run
  • Photos never leave the iPhone, so the listing can say "Data Not Collected"

One switch fixed the unit economics, the security risk and the privacy story at once, and privacy became a selling point in the listing.

04

Four releases in five weeks

Less than three weeks after launch, version 1.2 added a Brew Log (method, dose, grind, yield, time, rating), a coffee-remaining estimate that updates with every brew, iCloud photo sync, a finer 1–100 grind scale and prices in the user's own currency.

Reviewing the code before release, I found a serious bug: brew logs were filed by a bean's position in the list. Adding a new bean shifted every log onto the wrong bean. I gave each bean a permanent ID, migrated existing logs on first launch, and also fixed a sync issue that reverted edits after a relaunch.

Version 1.3 (18 November 2026) made the journal shareable. Bean cards turn a bag into one neat image (photo, roaster, origin, price, tasting notes, best recipe and rating) to send on Messages, WhatsApp or Instagram, while the journal itself stays private. Editing or repeating a brew now takes one tap, roasters have their own field that the label reader fills in, and each bean can be priced in the currency it was bought in.

I also changed the free tier. Instead of locking the Brew Log behind the paywall, free users can now log two brews per bean, so they try the paid feature before deciding to unlock it.

Behind the scenes, I moved builds from a cloud service into Xcode and put the project under version control, so every release is repeatable.

05

Marketing it

I wrote the App Store listing and ran the launch on Instagram myself: product carousels, feature posts, a coffee-pun character series and short Reels with on-screen callouts, planned in a content calendar.

06

What's next

Home Screen widgets and Shortcuts, so a brew can be logged without opening the app.
